# Example 1: Problem Validation

## Scenario

A SaaS company notices that new users are not completing onboarding.

The product team proposes building an AI onboarding assistant.

They believe this will help users complete setup faster.

---

## User Input

Problem:

Users need an AI assistant to complete onboarding faster.

Evidence:

- 15 customers requested AI assistance.
- Customer success team believes onboarding is confusing.
- Activation rate has decreased by 12% in the last quarter.

---

## PDR Analysis

## Problem vs Solution Check

The stated problem is actually a proposed solution.

The underlying problem hypothesis is:

> New users struggle to complete onboarding, causing lower activation.

---

## Evidence Assessment

Claim:

Users are struggling during onboarding.

Evidence:

- Activation rate decline indicates a potential onboarding issue.
- Customer feedback suggests friction exists.

Evidence limitations:

- Customer requests indicate interest in AI assistance, but do not prove AI is the correct solution.
- The root cause of onboarding failure is unclear.

---

## Problem Confidence

Medium.

The existence of onboarding friction is supported, but the cause is not yet understood.

---

## Critical Uncertainty

What specific part of onboarding causes users to fail?

Possible causes:

- Complex configuration
- Lack of guidance
- Missing information
- Poor workflow structure

---

## Recommendation

Do not move directly into building an AI onboarding assistant.

First investigate:

- Where users drop off in onboarding
- Which steps create friction
- Whether users need guidance, simplification, or automation

---

## Recommended Next Action

Analyse onboarding behaviour data and conduct targeted user research.

The goal is to identify the actual problem before selecting a solution.

---

## Human Validation Required

The product team should validate the problem interpretation with customer-facing teams and user evidence before committing to a solution.
